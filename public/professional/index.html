// LEVEL 0 — ORIGIN

Where the curiosity began

My grandfather was a civil engineer — he taught me to break big problems into solvable puzzles. My father is a farmer and a lecturer, and he showed me how knowledge and ground reality have to meet before anything meaningful happens. Between the two of them, I got a strange, useful inheritance: think in systems, but never forget who it's actually for.

I first started writing code on a Samsung E3 — a basic Android phone, no real dev tools, just curiosity and a willingness to break things. That curiosity turned physical in 9th grade: I built a production-grade automatic aquarium maintenance system — feeding, lighting, power backup — that ran independently for six-plus weeks during the COVID-19 lockdown. That's where IoT got under my skin.

"If something is important enough, you should try even if the probable outcome is failure."— a philosophy I borrowed from Elon Musk, and now actually live by
